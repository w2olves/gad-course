0. Before you start

  • What for: so every sign-up goes smoothly, with no surprises.
  • Cost: free.
  • When: before Day 0.
  1. An email address you can open right now. A Gmail address is the easiest: the same Google account also opens Gemini (section 1). One address for every course service means less confusion.
  2. Passwords. A different, long password for each site: 12 characters or more (three or four random words plus numbers work well). Write them in a paper notebook kept at home, or in a password manager. Never share them: not with friends, not with "support" in an email, not with the tutor in a chat. Real support never asks for a password or a code.
  3. Age. Every service sets its own minimum age, and the rules change, so check them on the service's own site. In October 2026 the official pages say:
    • Google: 13 to manage your own account (Google's general rule for countries not on its separate list; Ukraine isn't on it).
    • Gemini: 13 (or the age set in your country) with a personal Google account. Accounts managed with Family Link don't work in the Gemini web app.
    • ChatGPT: 13; under 18 you need a parent's or guardian's permission.
    • Epic Games and Fab: if you're under 13 (or under your country's age of digital consent, if that's higher), the account becomes a restricted Cabined Account until a parent or guardian gives consent by email.
    If you're under 18, show this page to a parent or guardian and sign up together wherever a service asks for their consent.
  4. Verification emails sometimes land in the Spam folder or, in Gmail, in the Promotions tab. No email after 5 minutes? Look there.
  5. Official sites only. Open the addresses on this page and check the address bar: google.com, chatgpt.com, epicgames.com, unrealengine.com, fab.com, blender.org, krita.org. No "free" copies of paid packs or programs from other sites.
  6. Windows prompts. While installing, Windows may ask "Do you want to allow this app to make changes to your device?". Click Yes only for an installer you downloaded from an official site on this page.

✅ It worked if: you have an email you can open, a safe place for passwords, and you know where to look for emails.

🆘 If something goes wrong: forgot your email password? On the sign-in page click "Forgot password?" and follow the prompts.

1. Google account and Gemini (your tutor)

  • What for: you paste your lessons into Gemini every day: it's your tutor.
  • Cost: the free plan is enough.
  • When: Day 0, then every day.

Google account

  1. Open accounts.google.com/signup. (Google's help: Create a Google Account.)
  2. Enter your First name; the surname is optional (Last name (optional)). Click Next.
  3. Enter your Birthday and Gender, then Next. Use your real date of birth: it decides which services open for you.
  4. Pick one of the suggested Gmail addresses, or Create your own Gmail address, then Next.
  5. Create a password and type it again, then Next. Write the password down.
  6. Phone number: Google may ask for one to verify you (an SMS code arrives) or let you Skip it.
  7. Review your details, Next, read Privacy and Terms, then I agree. Your account page opens.
  8. Recommended: turn on 2-Step Verification at myaccount.google.com/security.

Gemini

  1. Open gemini.google.com. If you see Sign in at the top right, click it and sign in with your Google account.
  2. A new chat: the New chat button at the top left (can't see it? Click Menu ☰ first).
  3. To paste a lesson: on the day page click Copy lesson, click the message box at the bottom of Gemini, press Ctrl+V, then Enter (or the send arrow). Every lesson goes in a new chat.
  4. First check: type "Привіт! Відповідай українською." and send it.

📌 Course rule: a normal chat. Don't turn on Guided Learning or Canvas (they're in the Tools menu next to the message box). If the message box shows a chip for one of these tools, remove it with its ✕ and open a new chat.

✅ It worked if: Gemini answers in a new chat and your chats start to appear in the list on the left.

🆘 If something goes wrong:

  • The free limit ran out (Gemini says you've reached a limit): do this lesson in ChatGPT (section 2) by pasting the same lesson text into a new chat there. Gemini's limit resets later.
  • Gemini won't open with this account: check the birthday on your Google account and that it isn't a Family Link account (Gemini's requirements). Use ChatGPT meanwhile.
  • No SMS arrives: check the number and country code (+380 for Ukraine), wait a few minutes and ask for the code again.

🔒 Safety: never paste passwords, codes, document numbers or your home address into a chat.

3. Epic Games account

  • What for: one account opens the Epic Games Launcher, Unreal Engine and Fab with its free packs.
  • Cost: free.
  • When: Day 0.
  1. Open epicgames.com/id/register. (You can also create the account inside the Launcher with Create account, section 4.)
  2. Enter your Date of birth, then Continue.
  3. Fill in your email, first name, last name, Display Name and password. The Display Name is public and other people see it: better not to use your full real name.
  4. Tick the box for the Terms of Service (Epic's terms and the Epic Games Store EULA), then Continue. The boxes for news and marketing emails are optional.
  5. Open your email: Epic sends a numeric code. Enter it, then Verify email.
  6. Read the Privacy Policy, then Continue. Done.
  7. Two-factor authentication (recommended): open epicgames.com/account/password (Password and security), and under Two-Factor Authentication click Set up next to the method you want; enter the code sent by email, SMS or your authenticator app. A confirmation email follows. Methods: an authenticator app (such as Google Authenticator or Microsoft Authenticator), an email code, SMS (not supported in every region), or Epic Authenticator in Epic's mobile app. (Epic's 2FA help.)

✅ It worked if: you can sign in at epicgames.com, and Password and security shows your 2FA method as active.

  • SMS says "SMS to this region is not supported": choose an authenticator app or email instead.
  • The account became a Cabined Account (if the age entered is under 13 or under the age of digital consent): without a parent's or guardian's consent, new downloads and some Unreal Engine features don't work. The parent gets an email and gives consent (Cabined Accounts).

🔒 Safety: 2FA protects the account even if someone learns your password. Never tell anyone your 2FA codes.

4. Epic Games Launcher and Unreal Engine 5.8

  • What for: Unreal Engine is the course's main program; the Launcher installs and updates it.
  • Cost: free.
  • When: Day 0. Start the download the evening before (about 40 GB).
  1. Open unrealengine.com/download and click Download Launcher. The installer (an .msi file) saves to your Downloads folder.
  2. Double-click it, click Yes if Windows asks, then Install, and wait until it finishes.
  3. Open the Epic Games Launcher and Sign In with your Epic account: email, password, and the 2FA code if it's on. You can't download the engine without signing in.
  4. Click Unreal Engine on the left, then the Library tab at the top.
  5. Click the + next to ENGINE VERSIONS: an engine tile appears. Click the version number on the tile and choose 5.8 (the newest 5.8.x). Even if a newer version is shown by default, choose 5.8: the course is built for it.
  6. Click Install, read Unreal Engine Pricing and click Accept, then read the End User License Agreement, tick the box and click Accept.
  7. In Choose install location, click Browse and pick a fast drive (an SSD) with space. The engine takes about 40 GB; the Options window shows the exact size.
  8. Options: keep the ticks as they are. Core Components are required, and Templates and Feature Packs are needed for the Third Person template. Click Apply.
  9. Click Install. Progress shows on the tile and under Downloads on the left; you can pause and resume the install.
  10. When it's done, the tile shows Launch. The first start is slow because Unreal prepares shaders. Don't close the window, even if progress seems stuck.

ℹ️ Starter Content in 5.8. Since version 5.7, Epic no longer includes Starter Content with the Launcher's engine, and the course doesn't need it: the Third Person template brings simple grey blocks (the LevelPrototyping folder, section 5), and section 6 lists the free packs the course uses. Don't download Starter Content from other sites.

✅ It worked if: the 5.8 tile shows a Launch button, and launching opens the Unreal Project Browser.

🆘 If something goes wrong:

  • Not enough space: free up the drive or pick another one with Browse.
  • The download is stuck: pause, then resume; restart the Launcher; check your internet and free space.
  • The tile shows Update instead of Launch: a fix (HotFix) is out; click Update.
  • Broken files or odd errors: on the tile, the ▼ arrow, then Verify; the Launcher re-downloads only what's damaged.
  • The Launcher won't sign in: check your internet and the Windows date and time, turn off any VPN, restart the Launcher and the PC.
  • Unreal won't open or crashes: update your graphics driver from the card maker's official site (NVIDIA, AMD or Intel), restart the PC, then Verify.

🔒 Safety: download the Launcher only from unrealengine.com or epicgames.com. Epic's official guide: Install Unreal Engine.

5. Your Unreal project: IslandCourse

  • What for: your one Unreal project for the whole course: you practise in it from Day 6 and build your island in it from Day 26.
  • Cost: free.
  • When: Day 0.
  1. Launcher, Unreal Engine, Library, Launch on the 5.8 tile. The Unreal Project Browser opens.
  2. On the left choose the Games category, then the Third Person template.
  3. On the right, under Project Defaults: Blueprint (not C++); Target Platform Desktop; Quality Preset Maximum; leave Ray Tracing as it is.
  4. Project Location: a folder on a fast local drive, outside the synced course folder, for example D:\Unreal\. Project Name: IslandCourse.
  5. Click Create. The first opening is slow (shaders): let it finish.
  6. Check: the green Play button at the top, walk the mannequin with W A S D and look with the mouse, then Esc to stop.
  7. Switch on the Water plugin (an extra part of Unreal that adds oceans, lakes and rivers): Edit → Plugins → type Water in the search box → tick Water. If Unreal says the plugin is Beta or needs other plugins, click Yes. Then click Restart Now.

✅ It worked if: the mannequin runs around the level, the Content Drawer (Ctrl+Space) has a LevelPrototyping folder, and after the restart Edit → Plugins still shows Water ticked.

What the project already has

For blockouts, stand-ins and scale checks, the course uses what Unreal already has, plus the free packs in section 6:

  • LevelPrototyping (comes with the Third Person template): simple grey blocks SM_Cube, SM_ChamferCube, SM_Cylinder, SM_Plane, SM_Ramp, SM_QuarterCylinder and SM_QuarterCylinderOuter; grid materials M_PrototypeGrid, MI_PrototypeGrid_Gray and more; a door, SM_Door and SM_DoorFrame_Edge, in LevelPrototyping/Interactable/Door/Meshes.
  • Basic shapes: Cube, Sphere, Cylinder, Cone and Plane in the Place Actors panel, under Shapes. In the Content Browser they're in /Engine/BasicShapes once Show Engine Content is ticked in its Settings.
  • Water: the Water plugin you switch on in step 7 adds Water Body Lake, Water Body River and Water Body Ocean to Place Actors. On Day 29 you use them to build the ocean, a river, a lake and a waterfall.

📁 Folders in Content: Course/DayNN is for practice, Island/… is for the island. You create the level L_Island on Day 26. The folders appear as the day pages ask for them.

🆘 If something goes wrong: no LevelPrototyping folder? Then the project was made from another template: make a new one from Games, Third Person. The window looks different? Screenshot and ask your tutor.

6. Fab, Poly Haven and free packs

  • What for: free 3D packs for your levels.
  • Cost: the course uses free packs only; don't buy anything.
  • When: from Day 24, then whenever you need them until the end of the course.
  1. Open fab.com, click Sign In at the top right and sign in with your Epic account (the same one as in the Launcher).
  2. Find the pack: with the search, or from a link on the day page (for example from free-packs-list.md).
  3. Check that it really is free: the price on the right shows Free (or 0) on every licence option. A crossed-out price with a percentage (for example -50%) is a discount, not a free pack. In doubt? Don't add it, and ask.
  4. Read the licence (License terms). Standard License: you can use it in your own projects; the UE-Only Content label means "only in Unreal Engine projects". CC-BY (Creative Commons Attribution): you can use it, but you must credit the author: write the name, author, licence and link in credits.md.
  5. Click Add to My Library. The first time, a window with Fab's agreement (EULA) appears: read it and agree; the news box is optional.
  6. Launcher, Unreal Engine, Library, scroll down to Fab Library and search for the pack by name. The new pack isn't there? Click Refresh or restart the Launcher.
  7. The pack's tile shows one of these buttons:
    • Add to Project (asset packs): choose IslandCourse, then Add to Project. If IslandCourse isn't in the list, tick Show all projects and pick the newest version the pack offers.
    • Create Project (complete projects, like Electric Dreams in section 7).
    • Install to Engine (plugins).
  8. When the download finishes, open IslandCourse and the Content Drawer: the pack's new folder is there.
  9. Packs that only come as FBX or glTF (no Add to Project button): on fab.com open the pack (or My Library), click Download (the download icon), choose the format; the archive saves to Downloads; right-click it, Extract All. In Unreal: Content Drawer, Import (or drag the files into the window), Import All. Import into your day folder (Course/DayNN).
  10. Another route is Fab inside Unreal: the Window menu, Fab (or the Fab button next to + Add in the Content Drawer), the My Library icon; the + at the bottom right of a tile adds the pack to the open project (Epic's help).

🌿 Poly Haven is another allowed free source: textures, HDRI skies and models at polyhaven.com, no account needed, licence CC0. Download the file, import it into your day folder (Course/DayNN) and still write a line in credits.md.

✅ It worked if: the pack is in My Library on fab.com, in Fab Library in the Launcher, and shows as a folder in the Content Drawer.

🆘 If something goes wrong:

  • The pack isn't in Fab Library: check that fab.com and the Launcher are signed in to the same account; click Refresh.
  • The Add to Project window looks different or no version fits: screenshot and ask your tutor.
  • The download stands still: Downloads in the Launcher, pause, then resume.

🔒 Safety: don't buy anything and never enter card details. If the site asks for payment, it's the wrong pack.

7. Electric Dreams (download from Day 24, work from Day 34)

  • What for: Epic's free sample, a jungle built with PCG. It is a separate project, ElectricDreamsSample: you read how its PCG works and migrate its forest into your IslandCourse.
  • Cost: free (Standard License, Unreal Engine only).
  • When: Day 24: start the download first thing (it takes hours); about 57 to 60 GB. You start working with it on Day 34 (weeks 7 to 12).
  1. If your PC has 16 GB of memory, first raise the Windows page file to 32 GB or more (Epic lists 32 GB of RAM for this sample; with 16 GB, open only its small levels): Win+R, sysdm.cpl, Enter, the Advanced tab, Performance: Settings, the Advanced tab, Virtual memory: Change; untick Automatically manage paging file size for all drives, pick your SSD, Custom size: Initial size 32768, Maximum size 49152 (these are MB), Set, OK, and restart the PC.
  2. Open Electric Dreams Env on Fab, check that it says Free, then Add to My Library.
  3. Launcher, Unreal Engine, Library, Fab Library, search "Electric Dreams", Create Project. The sample only shows in Fab Library once a compatible engine version is installed (yours is 5.8).
  4. Name: ElectricDreamsSample. Folder: a fast local SSD outside the synced course folder, with at least three times the download size free (about 57 to 60 GB, so roughly 180 GB free; the Launcher shows the exact size). Version: 5.8, then Create.
  5. While it downloads (Downloads), you can work on the day's other tasks.
  6. First opening: Launcher, Library, My Projects, ElectricDreamsSample. Unreal compiles shaders for a long time, sometimes more than an hour. Don't close the editor; close the browser to free memory.
  7. The Startup level opens with on-screen help. The other levels are in the Content Browser under Content, Levels; open only the ones the day page names.
  8. On a 16 GB PC, never open ElectricDreams_Env: it's the whole 4 x 4 km environment, and Epic marks it as very resource-intensive (ElectricDreams_PCG too).

Links, in English: Epic's Electric Dreams guide and Epic's free course Electric Dreams Environment Unpacked.

✅ It worked if: ElectricDreamsSample opens on the Startup level and the Compiling Shaders counter at the bottom right is gone.

🆘 If something goes wrong:

  • Electric Dreams isn't in Fab Library: check that 5.8 is installed and that you added the pack with the same account; click Refresh.
  • Not enough space: pick another drive or free up this one.
  • The editor is slow or crashes: close other programs, check the page file, set Scalability to Medium (Settings, Engine Scalability Settings) and set Screen Percentage to 50% in the Viewport Options menu (top left of the viewport), as Epic advises for lower-spec PCs. The sample needs DirectX 12 and an up-to-date graphics driver.

8. Blender, Krita and other programs

  • What for: Blender for 3D models; Krita for painting, textures and photo work.
  • Cost: free, no account needed.
  • When: Day 0 (GIMP, Inkscape and Photopea are optional).

Blender

  1. Open blender.org/download and click Download Blender (for Windows it's an .msi installer of about 350 MB). Take the newest stable 5.x the site shows.
  2. Run the file: Next, accept the licence, Next, Install, Yes if Windows asks, Finish.
  3. Open Blender from the Start menu: a welcome window with the version number should appear.

Krita

  1. Open krita.org/en/download, click Windows Installer, run the file: Yes, Next … Install, Finish.
  2. The free Krita is only on krita.org. The Microsoft Store, Steam and Epic Games Store versions are paid (the money funds development), so there's no need to buy one for the course.

Alternatives

  • GIMP: gimp.org/downloads, then "Download GIMP … directly" and run the installer.
  • Inkscape (vector graphics): inkscape.org/release, then Windows, 64-bit, and the installer (.msi or .exe).
  • Photopea: photopea.com runs in your browser, with nothing to install and no account needed (the free version shows ads).

✅ It worked if: Blender and Krita both open from the Start menu.

🆘 If something goes wrong: Windows shows a warning about the file? Check that you downloaded it from an official site on this page; if in doubt, delete it and download it again from there. Blender won't open? Update your graphics driver.

9. Wikimedia Commons (reference photos)

  • What for: real photographs of the places and objects you build.
  • Cost: free, no account needed.
  • When: from Day 1; most of all on Day 21, when you collect 15 photos of your place.
  1. Open commons.wikimedia.org and use the Search Wikimedia Commons box. English words work best (for example "Odesa harbour" or "quay bollard").
  2. Click a photo: its file page opens (the title starts with File:).
  3. Scroll to the Licensing section: the licence and the author are there.
  4. Use CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC0 or Public domain. Skip anything marked NC (NonCommercial): Commons doesn't accept such files, but you'll meet them on other sites.
  5. Above the photo click Download, choose the largest size (the original) and save it. Another route is the Original file link under the photo.
  6. Add a line to credits.md: file name, author, licence and the link to the file page. For example: Day01 | File:… | author … | CC BY-SA 4.0 | https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:…

✅ It worked if: the photo is in your day folder and credits.md has a line for it.

🆘 If something goes wrong: no Download button? Use the Original file link under the photo. The licence is unclear? Don't use that photo.

10. OpenStreetMap (maps)

  • What for: a map of your place, at scale, for the plot plan.
  • Cost: free, no account needed.
  • When: from Day 21 (your real place), then whenever you need it.
  1. Open openstreetmap.org, type a town or street in the search box at the top left, click Go and click a result.
  2. Zoom to the area you need with the mouse wheel.
  3. Click the Share button on the right (the share icon): a panel opens.
  4. Under Image: Format PNG; Scale sets the map scale (for example 1 : 2000), or tick Set custom dimensions and drag a frame on the map around your area.
  5. Click Download: the file saves to Downloads. Move it into your day folder.
  6. Credit: "© OpenStreetMap contributors" on the image or next to it, and a line in credits.md (OpenStreetMap's rules).

✅ It worked if: the map PNG is in your day folder and the credit is in credits.md.

🆘 If something goes wrong: Download gives an error? Make the area or the scale smaller and try again.

  • What for: your island's terrain, taken from a real place.

14. Google Drive for desktop (handing in your work)

  • What for: the course folder lives on Google Drive. Google Drive for desktop syncs it to your PC, and your work reaches the course coordinator automatically.
  • Cost: free (you need the Google account from section 1).
  • When: Day 0.
  1. Open google.com/drive/download and download Drive for desktop for Windows.
  2. Run the installer you downloaded, click Yes if Windows asks, and wait until it finishes.
  3. Click Sign in with browser, sign in with the Google account that has access to the course folder, and confirm.
  4. A Google Drive icon appears near the clock, and File Explorer gets a Google Drive drive with a My Drive folder.

✅ It worked if: File Explorer shows Google Drive → My Drive → the course folder, and COURSE.html opens in your browser when you double-click it.

🆘 If something goes wrong:
